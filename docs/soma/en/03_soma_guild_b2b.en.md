## Overview & Purpose

This module defines the specifications of **SOMA Guild**, an autonomous B2B production network that reorganizes highly skilled IT and specialist professionals who dropped out of conventional synchronous workplaces into friction-free asynchronous units, clearing enterprise stagnant tasks (technical debt) via a 24-hour time-zone relay driven by Earth's rotation. It formalizes the dry governance protocol (3-stage selection filter) blocking emotional labor, the bounded automated task decomposition function $\Phi(\text{Task}_{\text{stagnant}})$ triggered by consecutive SOS invocations, and the SATORI integration pipeline computing the shortcut possibility score $\text{SC}_{\text{score}}$ from 3-layer de-contextualized failure logs (FDR-V2).

## 3.1 Dropout Elite Re-allocation & "24/7 Asynchronous Rotation Relay"

SOMA Guild reorganizes highly skilled IT and specialized professionals—such as software engineers, designers, and workflow analysts who dropped out of conventional employment due to an inability to adapt to traditional work environments (synchronous work schedules, forced physical presence, rigid deadline negotiations, and interpersonal friction)—into non-synchronous, friction-free production units.

Stagnant enterprise tasks (legacy code refactoring, unmaintained system documentation, and unstructured data cleansing) are acquired from corporate clients under bulk umbrella contracts (e.g., JPY 100,000 / 3-month delivery window). Upon intake, internal AI Directors (LLM Orchestrators) automatically deconstruct and re-chunk these contracts into micro-execution units ("Quests," such as 50 units valued at JPY 2,000 each) and immediately inject them into a shared task storage pool.

* **Engine A (Recovering Experts in Advanced Nations):** Highly skilled professionals experiencing fluctuating physical or mental health who cannot endure long, rigid shifts (e.g., professionals on leave or separated in Japan, Europe, and North America). They handle high-difficulty stagnant tasks with loose deadlines, accessing the pool randomly during daytime hours when feeling well to execute debugging, refactoring, and proofreading.

* **Engine B (Active Elite Talent in Quasi-Advanced Nations):** Healthy, high-responsiveness young professionals (e.g., Eastern Europe, Southeast Asia). They execute time-critical standard B2B development and design tasks, maintaining high utilization driven by compensation rates substantially above local market averages. Tasks submitted overnight by enterprises in advanced economies are processed steadily during their daytime hours.

The interface between both engineering pools is restricted strictly to text, eliminating $100\%$ of interpersonal emotional labor (meetings, subtle social cues, phone calls). If a worker experiences a sudden health deterioration, pressing a single "SOS Button"—without writing excuses or apologies—immediately returns the task to the pool penalty-free, automatically passing the baton to an active member in another time zone.

## 3.2 Dry Governance & 3-Stage Selection Pipeline

To preserve production precision and operational safety while filtering out aggressive or emotionally dependent behaviors ("demands for special empathy," hostility, or silent abandonment), SOMA Guild enforces an automated 3-stage dry governance pipeline:

* **Automated Task Recall & Re-Chunking Engine on Consecutive SOS:** If a task triggers "3 consecutive SOS withdrawals" or records "zero completion progress within 24 hours of pool injection," the AI Director automatically locks and recalls the task as an anomaly (`Stagnant Task`). LLM analysis decomposes the recalled unit into finer sub-chunks (e.g., $50 \times \text{JPY } 2,000 \to 200 \times \text{JPY } 500$) while generating standardized Q&A specification clarifications before re-injecting it into the pool.

## 3.3 Enterprise Task Cleansing & "3-Step B2B Onboarding Model"

To eliminate psychological barriers to enterprise adoption, SOMA Guild deploys a progressive 3-stage onboarding pipeline:

1. **Step 1: Front-End (Free / Low-Cost Trash Diagnosis & Spot Cleanup):** AI isolates and cleanses a subset of stagnant client logs or repositories at zero cost, immediately quantifying the scale of technical debt.

2. **Step 2: Middle-End (Visualized Health Map & Bottleneck Report):** Delivers a structured dependency graph and bottleneck financial impact map across the enterprise system.

3. **Step 3: Back-End (Subscription Maintenance & "External Digital General Affairs"):** Transitions to recurring monthly retainer contracts for continuous refactoring and documentation maintenance. Simultaneously, anonymized process execution logs undergo Triple-Monetization across HR Tech, AI Training Data, and Consulting Research markets.

### 2) Game Theory & Incentives

Payoff matrix $(U_{\text{worker}}, U_{\text{guild}})$ for the SOS Moral Hazard Prevention & Handover Game:

| Action Choice | $U_{\text{worker}}$ (Worker Payoff) | $U_{\text{guild}}$ (Guild Payoff) | 
| ----- | ----- | ----- | 
| $A_1$**: Complete Task** | $R_{\text{task}} - C_{\text{effort}} + \Delta \text{Score}_{\text{trust}}$ | $V_{\text{deliverable}} - R_{\text{task}}$ | 
| $A_2$**: Legitimate SOS Withdrawal** | $0 - D_{\text{stake\_lock}} + \text{Rebated}_{\text{on\_validation}}$ | $0$ (Re-chunked & returned to pool) | 
| $A_3$**: Malicious Spam Drop / Abandonment** | $-D_{\text{stake\_slashed}} - \Delta \text{Score}_{\text{penalty}}$ | $-C_{\text{delay\_loss}}$ | 

* **Equilibrium Condition:** Repeated defaults or malicious task hoarding ($A_3$) trigger immediate forfeiture of staked trust deposits ($-D_{\text{stake\_slashed}}$) and trust-score deductions, whereas prompt early SOS notifications due to genuine health fluctuations ($A_2$) incur zero penalty. Consequently, either completing the task or immediately passing the baton via SOS dominates forcing degraded quality or silent abandonment.

### 3) Mathematical Formulae

#### Formula 3.1: Shortcut Possibility Score ($\text{SC}_{\text{score}}$) Coupling Equation

Probabilistic score $\text{SC}_{\text{score}} \in (0, 1)$ combining cosine similarity between dimensionless parameter vectors $\mathbf{r}$ and expired patent graphs $\mathcal{G}_{\text{patent}}$ with technological advancement gaps $\Delta \text{Tech}_{\text{gap}} \ge 0$:

$$
\text{SC}_{\text{score}} = \sigma\left( w_1 \cdot \text{Similarity}(\mathbf{r}, \mathcal{G}_{\text{patent}}) + w_2 \cdot \ln\left(1 + \Delta \text{Tech}_{\text{gap}}\right) - w_3 \cdot \epsilon_{\text{noise}} - b_0 \right)
$$

$$
\sigma(x) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-x}}
$$

* $\text{Similarity}(\mathbf{r}, \mathcal{G}_{\text{patent}}) \in [-1, 1]$: Structural cosine similarity against expired patent and academic knowledge graphs.

* $\Delta \text{Tech}_{\text{gap}} \ge 0$: Advancement multiplier in compute and LLM reasoning capacity between historical failure occurrence and the present.

* $\epsilon_{\text{noise}} \ge 0$: Information dilution penalty introduced by differential privacy noise injection.

* $w_1, w_2, w_3 > 0, \; b_0 \in \mathbb{R}$: SATORI calibration weight constants.

#### Formula 3.2: Bounded Automated Task Decomposition Function $\Phi(\text{Task}_{\text{stagnant}})$

Triggered when consecutive SOS invocations satisfy $N_{\text{sos}} \ge 3$ or upon 24-hour stagnation, determining chunk count $M \in [2, M_{\max}]$ with zero-division and divergence guards:

$$
M = \min\left( M_{\max}, \; \max\left( 2, \; \left\lceil \frac{\text{Budget}_{\text{time\_orig}}}{\max\left(\text{Target}_{\text{unit\_time}}, \; 15\text{ min}\right)} \times \left( 1 + \alpha_{\text{difficulty}} \cdot N_{\text{sos}} \right) \right\rceil \right) \right)
$$

$$
\Phi(\text{Task}_{\text{stagnant}}) \to \left\{ \text{Chunk}_1, \text{Chunk}_2, \dots, \text{Chunk}_M \right\}
$$

* $\text{Budget}_{\text{time\_orig}} \ge 15\text{ min}$: Baseline estimated duration of the parent task.

* $\max(\text{Target}_{\text{unit\_time}}, 15\text{ min})$: $15$-minute lower-bound limiter preventing zero-division and infinite micro-fragmentation ($M \to \infty$).

* $\alpha_{\text{difficulty}} = 0.25$: Difficulty adjustment multiplier scaled by consecutive SOS count.

* $M_{\max} = 500$: Hard upper ceiling on micro-chunks generated from a single parent contract.

### 4) Anti-Fragile Red-Teaming

1. **Attack Vector 3-A: Pool Exhaustion DDoS via Consecutive SOS & Low-Quality Submissions**

   * **Attack Scenario:** Malicious accounts claim tasks in bulk and repeatedly trigger SOS at the last minute, or submit LLM-generated garbage code to stall B2B delivery schedules.

   * **Defensive Countermeasures:** Concurrent claim slots are capped according to trust scores (initial maximum of $1\text{--}2$ slots), and deliverables failing automated CI/CD unit tests post-F2 screening are immediately rejected with trust deposit slashing.

2. **Attack Vector 3-B: Corporate Re-Identification Attack from FDR-V2 Failure Logs**

   * **Attack Scenario:** Adversaries cross-reference published cleansed failure logs against patent filings and corporate press releases to identify originating enterprises for reputational attacks.

   * **Defensive Countermeasures:** Dimensionless parameter normalization, multi-quarter random timestamp shifting, and $\epsilon \le 0.1$ differential privacy injection reduce single-enterprise re-identification probability to information-theoretic bounds.
